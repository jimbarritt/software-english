# Portfolio Journal structure: a local reference cache for Software English

## Canonical sources

- Janz, T. (1982), "Initial comparisons of patterned behavior-based interviews versus unstructured interviews," Journal of Applied Psychology, 67(5), 577-580: https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1989-98087-011
- Julia Evans, "Get your work recognized: write a brag document" (2019): https://jvns.ca/blog/brag-documents/

Janz's paper is the traceable academic origin of behavioral
interviewing. It names the acronym SHARE, not STAR. STAR is a later,
widely used adaptation with no single publisher of record. The
brag-document practice has one clear originating essay (Evans, 2019),
as a practitioner convention rather than a formal standard.

## Entry structure (STAR)

Each entry records one accomplishment in four parts, in this order:

1. **Situation**: the context.
2. **Task**: the goal or the responsibility.
3. **Action**: the step taken.
4. **Result**: the outcome, with a metric where one exists.

## Journal conventions (from the brag-document practice)

- The journal is updated every two weeks, or in one long session per
  year.
- Each entry states its impact directly. Evans: "don't make your work
  sound better than it is, make it sound exactly as good as it is."
- Entries group under sections such as goals, projects, collaboration
  and mentorship, documentation, and what was learned.

## Common variants

- Janz's original model uses the acronym SHARE, not STAR.
- Some guides add a fifth field, Reflection, after Result.
