## What an explanation document is

Diátaxis names four documentation types: tutorials, how-to guides, technical reference, and explanation. It places these four in a systematic relationship and organises documentation around the structure of the need each type serves.

An explanation is understanding-oriented. Diátaxis defines it as "a discursive treatment of a subject, that permits reflection." It does not take the user's eye-level view, as in a how-to guide, or a close-up view of the machinery, as in reference material. Instead, it addresses questions of the form "can you tell me about…?"

Explanation deepens and broadens a reader's understanding of a topic. It can discuss the "bigger picture", make connections between topics, and unfold "the machinery's internal secrets, to help understand why something does what it does." Unlike reference material, explanation can include background, context, history, alternatives, opinion, and multiple perspectives.

## Structural conventions

Diátaxis gives concrete writing guidance for explanation:

- Provide context and background for a topic. Discuss why things are the way they are, not only what they are.
- Make connections to other topics and areas. Explanation can draw links that a task-focused or reference document does not.
- Discuss choices, alternatives, and justifications. Explanation can compare approaches and give reasons for a design or decision.
- Consider multiple perspectives and counter-examples where relevant.
- Give the document a title that has an implicit "about" in front of it, for example "About user authentication".
- Keep explanation closely bounded to its subject. It should not absorb instructional or technical content that belongs in a tutorial, how-to guide, or reference document.
- Explanation does not need to be task-focused or step-based. It is not bound to the ordering constraints of a tutorial or a how-to guide.

## How this differs from reference

Reference material is information-oriented: it describes the machinery of a system, and Diátaxis requires it to be austere and consistent, structured around the product rather than around a user's task or curiosity. Explanation is understanding-oriented and discursive: it steps back from the machinery to discuss context, connections, and reasoning, and it can include alternatives and opinion, which reference material excludes.
